### Why do I choose to take the exam?

The reason I wanted to study for this certification was to learn more about on-premises IT infrastructure. I wanted to understand more about how to set up and maintain a local network for businesses. I was also inspired by my job in IT support. When I first started this job in late March this year, I felt that my hardware knowledge was inadequate. My manager recommended this certification to me, and that's how it came onto my radar.

Therefore, to improve my IT knowledge and continue developing my career, I decided to take the exam. Most importantly, I am in a very "LinkedIn" state of my life HAHA.

### 1. Study Schedule

Unlike university, self-study doesn't give me a timetable or weekly lectures to keep me on track. So, I created an Excel study plan based on an open-source resource that divided the exam content into sections. Taking into account the suggested study time and my full-time job, I planned out 17 weeks of study, including revision and exam weeks. Though later I extended the study period for another 5 weeks due to other life commitments.

I used [Professor Messer's Network+ Training Course](https://www.professormesser.com/network-plus/n10-009/n10-009-video/n10-009-training-course/) for my content overview. I found the video durations and section breakdowns especially useful for planning my study at my own pace. The visuals also made concepts like different types of cables much easier to understand.

I also kept taking my own notes while watching the videos. Making your own notes helps turn new information into something you can actually recall during revision. Plus, when you forget something, you can't blame anyone but yourself for being forgetful!

### 3. Study Strategies

There are a few things that I think are important when preparing for this exam:

•	**Don't worry if you don't remember everything after your first run through the content.** After completing the content overview for the first time, you probably can only remember 50–60% of what you studied, and that's okay. Don't feel too discouraged when you get a low score on your practice exam.

•	**Use AI for concept understanding.** They literally know everyting! My favourite is ChatGPT, but you can use whichever tool you feel comfortable with.

•	**Redo the practice exam immediately after reviewing all the questions.** This is a tough one because you probably already feel tired after finishing a set of questions and reviewing them. But it is so important for building your muscle memory around the questions you got wrong and helping you get them right. For me, it also really built my confidence when I saw my score improve from 50% to 90% after redoing the questions. It's nice to give your brain some incentives.

•	**Don't be afraid to reschedule your exam date.** It is free, and if life happens, it happens. One of the best things about taking a non-university exam is the flexibility you have with your time.

•	**Find study resource for practicing command prompt skills.** This is something that I overlooked, while compTIA Network+ is mostly multiple choice questions, it actually has about four to five questions that are related to command prompt using, the one that almost tripped me was related to spanning-tree and vlan commands.


### 4. On The Day Of The Exam

They are something I always do on the day of an exam:
•	Do a set of new questions one or two hours before the exam.
•	Do a set of questions that I have done before one or two hours before the exam.
•	Be there 30 minutes early. Give yourself some extra time to check out the room. It never hurts to make yourself more comfortable in a new environment.


### 5. Trust yourself to have the ability to win, but also have the ability to lose. Just show up.
